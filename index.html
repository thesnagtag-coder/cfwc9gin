Rot and pests work quietly. You can spot the signs.

SHEET 00 · START

Timber rot and wood-boring pests usually start where timber stays damp and nobody looks: under floors, in roof spaces, behind cupboards and where wood touches soil. This check helps you find the visible signs, record where the moisture comes from, and track pests over time. You look, press gently and record. You never apply pesticides or treatments, and you never diagnose which rot or insect it is: a specialist does that.

Before you start - safety first

HSE
Rodent droppings: do not sweep or vacuum them. Ventilate for 30 minutes, wear gloves, and wet them with disinfectant before wiping up. (CDC Rodent clean-up)
Soft or crumbling timber: do not stand on, load or lean on soft floors, stairs, decks or balcony timbers. Keep people off until a specialist has checked them. (Professional practice)
  • Roof space and sub-floor: look from the hatch or opening only, with a torch and someone with you. Never step between joists. (Professional practice)
  • Probing exposed wood for hollow spots is useful, but in a home you do not own, ask first and press gently. (EPA Termites, Professional practice)
  • No sprays, powders or treatments: pest treatment is for a licensed professional. (EPA Termites)

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It does not identify which fungus or insect is present. Treatments differ, so a specialist must diagnose it.
    • It gives no pesticide or chemical treatment advice. Treatment is for a licensed professional.
    • It does not replace a timber pest inspection required or recommended in your country (for example, inspections to Australian Standard AS 3660).
    • It never asks you to walk on soft timber, step between joists or open up walls or floors.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • Termites: probe exposed wood for hollow spots; learn to tell termite swarms from ant swarms (termite wings roughly equal in length, straight antennae); keep soil at the foundation dry through grading and drainage; reduce openings; do not store firewood or wood debris next to the house; choose a licensed pest control company. (EPA Termites)
    • Rot: dry rot needs damp timber with a moisture content of around 20% that is freely accessible to air, and can spread through masonry; signs include cuboidal cracking, cotton-wool-like mycelium, fruiting bodies and spore dust. Wet rot stays more localised. Find and fix the moisture source first; the correct diagnosis matters because treatments vary. (Designing Buildings)
    • Wood-boring insects: exit holes and bore dust (frass) are the signs; old holes do not always mean an active attack; beetles are attracted to damp timber; professional inspection tells old from active. (Designing Buildings Woodworm)
    • Rodents: mice can get through a hole the width of a pencil, 6 mm (1/4 in); seal gaps around pipes and lines with steel wool and caulk, or metal for larger gaps; store food in sealed containers. Do not sweep or vacuum droppings: ventilate 30 minutes, wear gloves, wet with disinfectant for 5 minutes, then wipe up. (CDC Rodent seal-up, CDC Rodent clean-up)
    • Moisture: fix leaks and dry wet materials quickly; keep indoor humidity below 60%, ideally 30-50%. (EPA Mold & Moisture)
    • Units: 1 mm = 0.04 in; 6 mm = 1/4 in. Timber moisture in % from a meter on its timber setting, context only. Metric first, imperial in brackets.
    Country-specific: Australia manages termites under AS 3660.1 (new buildings) and AS 3660.2 (existing buildings), and timber pest inspections are common before buying. In the US, firms offering termite services must be licensed by the state. Species, risks and rules differ widely by country and climate: check your local pest regulator.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general inspection and HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.
